Product

Three layers, and they are at three different stages of being built. Naming that is the first honest thing this memo can do.

  • The terminal — a desk and a console for an operator holding capital on-chain. Live. An operator asks a question; an agent runs a five-node cycle; the answer, the forecast behind it and the guardrails that gated it all land in one surface.
  • The infrastructure beneath it — forecast (TimesFM-3 quantile projections in a ledger), decision (the agent cycle), risk (typed constraints that can force a re-plan), custody (a Safe owned by an embedded signer), settlement (Aqua/SwapVM, Uniswap v4, 1inch, Morpho). Built and running, with the gaps listed on page 3.
  • The instrument — the web3-aligned ETF wrapper. This is the market this is infrastructure for, and it is not built. It is named here so that no reader discovers the omission later.

What the product actually does, worked once

A strategy's yield falls under its floor, or its volatility breaches its cap, and the position flees. The volatile leg swaps to a stablecoin through a SwapVM program containing an instruction we wrote — YieldBandFlight at opcode 0xb3 — and the proceeds are deposited into a Morpho vault. The whole thing is two Aqua apps, deliberately separate: a router that is a canonical SwapVM plus our instruction, and a refuge app that "holds no pricing logic at all; its whole job is that one call moves both legs back to the maker, which removes the window of half-executed risk between the swap and the deposit."

That is the product's unit of work: an intervention calibrated to one moment, executed atomically, dissolved. The deck calls this product:moment fit, because that is the term the Machine Economy thesis uses for it and it is exactly what this is.

Why now

Any two of these inventions without the third, and you hit a ceiling. Fabric Ventures — the three converging inventions

Specialised accelerated compute means a quantile band per pool, per request, in under a second. Large language models mean an agent that can read a constraint set and propose a reallocation inside it. Programmable blockchains mean settlement with no intermediary and a receipt for every action — the audit trail is native to the substrate rather than bolted onto it.

The standards that close the loop have landed recently:

  • ERC-8004 — agent reputation portable across counterparties (mainnet, January 2026).
  • EIP-4337 — account abstraction; 26M smart wallets and counting.
  • Scale — $27.6T settled on-chain in 2024, rivalling Visa and Mastercard combined; and by May 2026 a Chainlink × Ark Invest report put autonomous agents at roughly 30% of TVL in top-tier DeFi pools.

That last figure is the reason this company should exist now rather than in 2022. The capital is already a third machine-managed. The tooling around it is not.

Competition

Brava Finance is the closest competitor and the deck names it. An institutional platform for allocating stablecoin and tokenized credit: real-time risk scoring, mandate automation to codify allocation policies, cross-chain execution across DeFi and RWA, audit-ready reporting, non-custodial execution from the allocator's wallet via Safe. That is our custody model and our pitch, largely.

The difference is two words, and both are checkable in the code:

  • Policy versus forecast. A mandate is a static rule. Ours is a quantile forecast with a confidence band that decides when the band is breached — q10/q50/q90 in a forecast ledger, with calibration measured against realised yield.
  • Reported versus required. Their audit is a report about decisions. Ours is a schema a decision cannot be written without: citations: z.array(...).min(1). An ungrounded answer degrades to an explicit HOLD marked ungrounded rather than passing quietly.

The honest positioning is therefore forecast-driven × structurally required, not "automated and accountable" — because that quadrant is not empty, and claiming it would be the first thing a careful reader checks and disproves. The third category of alternative, which pitches usually omit because it is unglamorous and correct, is non-consumption: a human with a multisig, a spreadsheet and a Telegram thread. That is the incumbent, it is free, and it is what this has to displace.

What we know is missing

Taken from the repository's own documentation, where it is already written this way. An investor who finds these unaided will trust everything above less.

  1. Nothing is fully committed yet. The work exists as reviewed changes rather than a clean history; 22 atomic commits are planned. Process, not engineering.
  2. Intent persistence is missing. The intent table carries the typed-data hash that binds a signature to the payload an operator actually reviewed, and nothing writes or reads it — so an intent's identifier is currently a caller-supplied handle rather than a reference to something that was reviewed.
  3. Custody caps are not enforced. Per-transaction and daily caps and the allowlist exist as schema, and nothing consults them. Enforcing them needs the store above.
  4. Position creation is unencoded, deliberately. The entry point is an actions batch whose command bytes could not be retrieved, so it was left rather than guessed — a wrong byte executes the neighbouring action.
  5. The ETF wrapper does not exist. It is the market, not the product.
  6. Two components render nowhere and the risk snapshot store is unconfigured on the deployed surface, which the product says out loud rather than hiding.
